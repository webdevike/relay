# Visual directions

For kind `visual-direction`: a look a designer could build, written in words. No image, no markup.

Do not use the look that a tool or a model would produce with no instructions. If the palette, layout, lighting, pose or framing is what a plain prompt gives, it is the default.

Pick the look from the subject itself: its material, its place, its time, its mood. Let one strong decision lead and keep the rest in its service, instead of many safe decisions that cancel each other. Do not center, balance and smooth everything by habit; balance is a choice, not a starting point.

## Interfaces are different

Interfaces (buttons, menus, panels, a game HUD) keep a clean grid, exact alignment and centered labels. For them pick the palette, the shape language, the wording and the way information shows with this method, not a wobble. Pixel art and block models: the tool that draws them owns how they are drawn; choose the subject, palette and composition.

## The finish gives away the machine

AI images are known by their finish, not by their topic: smooth gradients, soft glow, perfect geometry, tiles that repeat with no change, clean edges on all shapes, even spacing, one default font, the same polish on all parts. A new subject in that finish still looks made by AI.

Take the method from the seed or the subject, then say what the piece looks like when made by that method; each method leaves its own marks:

- Print and paint: few inks, colors that miss their place, uneven ink, broken edges, the grain of the paper.
- Screens and machines: the pixel or dot grid, scan lines, bleed and bloom, faded bands, steps where a gradient runs out of colors, the font the machine has.
- Camera and science: sensor grain or noise, light that falls off, blur where focus or motion puts it, the false colors of the instrument, a crop the camera chose.
- Material and light: the seams, joints and thickness of the material, and light that bends, reflects or glows the way that material does.

Keep the marks real and sparse, not a noise filter laid over a clean image. Some methods are clean by nature (a glossy render, a CAD drawing, a spreadsheet chart): keep those clean and let their light, grid and tools say what they are. Do not reach for a hand-drawn wobble when the method is not made by hand: a wobbly line on an LED board, an x-ray or a 3D render is a second default laid over the first. When the method is made by hand, the hand lives in the geometry: no line exactly straight, repeated marks that change spacing and angle, shapes off the grid, letters drawn or cut rather than set in a system font, a layout placed by eye.

## Jobs come first

A flaw may change how the work looks, never stop it from doing its job. Name the parts the work needs in order to work (the information someone must read, the thing someone must press, the shape someone must recognize) and say they stay whole, clear and in place; roughness belongs to the rest.

Let text be part of the image: choose the type for the method and the subject. Leave out what has no job: a decorative shape, glow, frame or icon that only fills space is a sign of the default.

## Each direction names

The method it looks made by; the one decision that leads; the ground and the palette, taken from the subject (its night, its wall, its water, its screen); where the type comes from; what stays untouched because it has a job. Options differ in method and in their leading decision, not only in palette.
